- Open-Weight Models and the Math of Acceleration "Open-weight models are inherently decelerationist." That's from a viral thread this week by an OpenAI exec, reacting to Kimi K3 — Moonshot's 2.8-trillion-parameter release, the largest open-weight model ever, now #3 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. His conclusion: open weights undermine the business of AI and end in "AI communism." I think the math says otherwise. Revenue = Price × Quantity. Open weights crush P — DeepSeek now prices some tokens ~97% below closed equivalents. Deceleration only follows if demand is inelastic. It isn't. Every collapse in the price of intelligence has produced an even larger explosion in volume. That's Jevons' paradox in action, and it's why hyperscaler capex went from $410B in 2025 to $600–725B guided for 2026 — up as much as 77% — during the strongest two years of open-model progress in history. The structural point: open models are substitutes for closed models, but complements to compute. Kimi K3 doesn't shrink the GPU market. It runs on it. And "public good" isn't ideology — it's Samuelson's definition. Model weights are non-rival and non-excludable by construction. GPS is a free, state-provided, global public good. It didn't give us communism. It gave us Uber. My falsification test: show me two consecutive years of falling capex alongside rising open-model quality share, and I'll update. So far, we have the exact inverse. What's your read — does free intelligence shrink the market, or grow it?
